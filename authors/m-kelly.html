The Last Harbour

A harbour under watch. A family under strain.

In a small northern town, fishing boats sit tied beneath floodlights while official notices multiply, soldiers arrive, and the word “temporary” begins to sound like a threat.

The Last Harbour follows Lars, a fisherman fighting to hold on to the only life he understands, as his family and community are pulled into something darker than a dispute over access, borders, or boats.

As the harbour closes, grief hardens into resistance. His son Elias is drawn towards the young men gathering in sheds and back rooms, while Sila, the youngest, records the town’s fear in drawings no adult wants to look at too closely.

Bleak, intimate, and tense, this is a story about occupation, fatherhood, silence, and the moment ordinary people realise that waiting is no longer neutral.

“If something is temporary, it should know when to stop.”

For readers who like their fiction cold, tense, and human.

This is not a cosy harbour story. It is a slow-burn descent into pressure: political, domestic, emotional, and moral.

Expect a close, character-led story with a bleak coastal atmosphere, family conflict, escalating occupation, and the painful cost of staying silent too long.

About M Kelly

M Kelly writes dark, character-driven fiction about pressure, loyalty, family, and the quiet moments before people are forced to choose who they are.
